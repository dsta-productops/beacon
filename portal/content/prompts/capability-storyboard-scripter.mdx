## What CLARA will ask you for

- **Programme name** — your programme's name (for example SKYPROTECT).
- **Track** — the part of the programme this document belongs to (for example a workstream, capability area or feature line), or `Programme-wide` if it spans several. The [CLARA page](/tools/clara) explains tracks.
- **Storyboard title** — short (for example "Tank-crew alerting under degraded comms").
- **Length** — the number of panels. Typically 8 to 12: fewer (5 to 6) for a quick brief, more (15 or more) for a full narrative.
- **Audience** — who the storyboard is for. Examples: operators reviewing the capability for the first time, or the engineering team scoping the build.
- **Inputs** — CLARA will search your programme's Confluence space for the operational scenario this storyboard shows, optionally the capability specification for the capability being shown, and optionally the persona shown in the storyboard. You can also paste in new material that is not in Confluence yet.

## Where the output lands

`Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Capability-storyboards/{{storyboard-title}}` inside your programme's Confluence space.

## Tips

- When you review CLARA's panels, check the carry-over field on each one. Without it you get a set of disconnected hero shots, not a story.
- If CLARA's panels read like a system diagram, push back. The capability should be visible in use.
- CLARA's panel-level prompts are the instructions for the visualisation tool, not the visualisation itself. Send them to Luma, Nano Banana or Forma to do the actual rendering.
