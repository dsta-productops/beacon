## What CLARA will ask you for

- **Programme name** — your programme's name (for example SKYPROTECT).
- **Track** — the part of the programme this document belongs to (for example a workstream, capability area or feature line), or `Programme-wide` if it spans several. The [CLARA page](/tools/clara) explains tracks.
- **PRD title** — short (for example "Incident-report capture v1").
- **Inputs** — CLARA will search your programme's Confluence space for the problem to build for, the problem statement and success criteria, the persona for the PRD, optionally the themes from the research synthesis, and the original request from stakeholders. You can also paste in new material that is not in Confluence yet.

## Where the output lands

`Knowledge Base/{{track}}/PRDs/{{prd-title}}` inside your programme's Confluence space.

## Tips

- CLARA asks clarifying questions when inputs are thin. Answer honestly, even if the answer is "I don't know yet". The Open questions section keeps a record of what is unknown.
- Treat CLARA's v0 PRD as a starting position, not a final document. Editing it by hand afterwards is normal, not the exception.

## Before you circulate: self-review checklist

Before sharing the PRD with stakeholders, walk through these checks. Most teams will fix gaps by editing the draft directly. A few questions bring up things the draft cannot fix on its own.

- **Problem statement** is one paragraph and describes a problem, not a solution in disguise. It says clearly who has the problem.
- **Success criteria** are measurable and describe what the capability or product must be able to do, and to what level. Avoid vague non-criteria such as "users will feel more confident".
- **Scope table** groups every relevant ranked problem into a module, or explicitly puts it under Out of scope. No ranked problem is left unaccounted for, and "Out of scope" names the rank and the reason.
- **User-story table** is in priority order. Every row's `ID · rank` ties back to the ranking, and every Evidence cell cites a real quote (verbatim) or observation with its source. Stories describe outcomes, not features.
- **Constraints and dependencies** name every outside thing the work depends on. Read it again with the question "what would block this?" in mind.
- **Open questions** are honest. The first draft is expected to be wrong in interesting ways, and the open questions are where you flag what you do not know.
- **Persona references** link to specific persona pages, never to an unnamed "the user".
