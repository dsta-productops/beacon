## What CLARA will ask you for

- **Programme name** — your programme's name (for example SKYPROTECT).
- **Track** — the part of the programme this document belongs to (for example a workstream, capability area or feature line), or `Programme-wide` if it spans several. The [CLARA page](/tools/clara) explains tracks.
- **Topic** — a short label for the interview topic (for example "operator decision-making under time pressure" or "shift handover friction"). It shapes the questions.
- **Interviewee** — role, seniority and the number of sessions planned.
- **Outcome question** — what the research needs to answer. Be specific: "do operators trust the alert system enough to act on it without a second confirmation?", not "how do operators feel about alerts".
- **Inputs** — CLARA will search your programme's Confluence space for a prior-knowledge summary on the topic (optional). You can also paste in new material that is not in Confluence yet.

## Where the output lands

`Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Interview-guides/{{topic}}` inside your programme's Confluence space.

## Tips

- The "Listening for" note on each question is the most useful part. It keeps the interviewer focused on what matters, instead of just collecting answers.
- Resist the temptation to add more questions. A guide with 8 sharp questions beats one with 20 vague ones. The "anything I should have asked" question at the end covers what you missed.
- Run this prompt again for each interviewee role, instead of building one giant guide for everyone. Different roles need different follow-up questions.
- After the interviews, the transcripts feed into the [research synthesiser](/prompts/research-synthesiser).
