## What CLARA will ask you for

- **Programme name** — your programme's name (for example SKYPROTECT).
- **Track** — the part of the programme this document belongs to (for example a workstream, capability area or feature line), or `Programme-wide` if it spans several. The [CLARA page](/tools/clara) explains tracks.
- **Operational scenario** — the page under `Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Operational scenarios/*`.
- **Mission task** — be specific: "detect, identify, and engage an inbound air contact from a frigate", or "neutralise a hostile armoured vehicle in urban terrain", not "air defence" or "ground operations".
- **Inputs** — CLARA will search your programme's Confluence space for the operational scenario for this capability, and the system context (the systems, platforms and communications involved). You can also paste in new material that is not in Confluence yet.

## Where the output lands

`Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Mission threads/{{mission-task}}` inside your programme's Confluence space.

## Tips

- When you review CLARA's thread, aim for 5 to 12 steps from start to finish. Too coarse misses decision points. Too fine documents screens. Push back if it lands outside that range.
- Look at the "failure mode + recovery" column first. The steps with the most expensive recovery paths are usually where investing in the capability pays off.
- Pair CLARA's output with a diagram from [Napkin AI](/tools/napkin-ai), so the data flow is visible at a glance.
