## What CLARA will ask you for

- **Programme name** — your programme's name (for example SKYPROTECT).
- **Track** — the part of the programme this document belongs to (for example a workstream, capability area or feature line), or `Programme-wide` if it spans several. The [CLARA page](/tools/clara) explains tracks.
- **Test type** — what kind of test this is. Examples: a usability test on an interactive prototype, a moderated walk-through of a clickable prototype with operators, an instrumented A/B test on a deployed feature, or a capability rehearsal in a scripted exercise.
- **Test name** — short (for example "Console-v1-usability-test" or "Tank-crew-alerting-rehearsal").
- **Test focus (optional)** — what this round of testing should cover. Leave it blank to test against all the success criteria of what is being tested. Otherwise narrow the scope to a specific user story (for example "submit-incident-report"), one or two success criteria, the features shipping in this planning interval (PI), or the storyboard beats being rehearsed.
- **Constraints (optional)** — time budget, limits on recruiting, environment, classification, secrecy.
- **Inputs** — CLARA will search your programme's Confluence space for the relevant documents and success criteria, and optionally field notes to draw scenarios from. You can also paste in new material that is not in Confluence yet.

## Where the output lands

`Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Test-plans/{{test-name}}` inside your programme's Confluence space.

## Tips

- The Objective sentence is the test of the test plan. CLARA proposes it before drafting the rest and waits for your confirmation. If it cannot be compressed into one line, that is a sign to narrow the test focus and run it again.
- When you review CLARA's scenarios, each should map to a success criterion. If a scenario cannot fail, it is not a test. Ask CLARA to rework it.
