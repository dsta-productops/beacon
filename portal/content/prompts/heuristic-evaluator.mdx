## What CLARA will ask you for

- **Programme name** — your programme's name (for example SKYPROTECT).
- **Track** — the part of the programme this document belongs to (for example a workstream, capability area or feature line), or `Programme-wide` if it spans several. The [CLARA page](/tools/clara) explains tracks.
- **Product name** — the product and the part of it under evaluation (for example "Oracle HCM — Absence & Onboarding").
- **Evaluation mode** — one of: a competitor's product, an existing system you want to improve, a system being retired, or a standalone evaluation. It decides how findings are attributed to their origin, and whether Appendix B of the report applies.
- **Task scenarios** — the one or two end-to-end tasks to walk through. If you do not give them, CLARA proposes them from the screens and asks you to confirm.
- **Inputs** — CLARA will search your programme's Confluence space for the product material to evaluate (which you supply), the task scenarios to walk through, the evaluation mode, and optionally a prior-knowledge summary on the product or area. You can also paste in new material that is not in Confluence yet.

## Where the output lands

`Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Heuristic-evaluations/{{product-name}}` inside your programme's Confluence space.

## Tips

- CLARA works with whatever level of detail you give it. A live link lets it try more states of the product than static screenshots. A PDF or deck is fine, but it freezes the product in one state. The Scope & limitations section of the report records this honestly.
- Treat the score as a way to compare, not as an absolute grade. It is most useful for tracking the same product across versions, or ranking several competitors that were evaluated in the same way.
- The findings are a minimum: a single evaluator will miss some problems. For a decision that matters, have a second evaluator repeat the exercise and merge the results. Nielsen's own research is the reason the report carries this caveat.
- In existing-system mode, the CFG-versus-PRD split and the achievable-via-fix index are the most persuasive parts of the report. They show the team how much it can fix by itself, now.

## Before you circulate: self-review checklist

- **Every finding names a specific screen and element.** No findings that state a principle in the abstract, and none that cannot be located on a screen.
- **Severity is justified.** Each rating reflects how often the problem occurs, how much it matters and how long it lasts, not just how annoying it felt. The worst ratings are for real catastrophes.
- **The score can be reproduced.** The deduction for each heuristic, the weights (which add up to 1.0) and the reasons for them are stated, so a reader can follow the score from the findings to the headline number. The band matches the number.
- **The achievable-via-fix index is present** (in existing-system and retirement modes), and the CFG/PRD attribution behind it matches Appendix B.
- **Strengths are recorded.** "What works well" names real strengths in each scenario, each citing a screen.
- **Scope and limitations are honest.** The caveats about static captures and a single evaluator are stated. The score is framed as an upper bound (the real score may be lower) and the findings as a lower bound (there may be more problems).
- **The walkthrough is complete.** Every finding in the register appears in full in a walkthrough, in the order the user meets it, with a stable ID. The appendix adds nothing new.
