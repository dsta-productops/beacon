## What CLARA will ask you for

- **Programme name** — your programme's name (for example SKYPROTECT).
- **Track** — the part of the programme this document belongs to (for example a workstream, capability area or feature line), or `Programme-wide` if it spans several. The [CLARA page](/tools/clara) explains tracks.
- **Operational scenario** — the page under `Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Operational scenarios/*` to base the specification on.
- **Capability name** — short (for example "Tank-crew alerting aid", "Fighter aircraft sensor-fusion module" or "Frigate surface-contact classifier").
- **Inputs** — CLARA will search your programme's Confluence space for the operational scenario for this capability, a capability brief or statement of operational need, and optionally known constraints (regulatory, integration, schedule or platform). You can also paste in new material that is not in Confluence yet.

## Where the output lands

`Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Capability specs/{{capability-name}}` inside your programme's Confluence space.

## Tips

- When you review CLARA's specification, every requirement should trace back to a specific beat in the scenario. Challenge any that cannot. That is where drift creeps in.
- Performance thresholds are where the draft will be vaguest, unless your operational scenario contains hard numbers. Push for measurable thresholds (such as "within 5 seconds, in 95% of cases") instead of "fast enough".
- Pair CLARA's output with a system diagram from [Napkin AI](/tools/napkin-ai), so the requirements have a structural picture to anchor to.
