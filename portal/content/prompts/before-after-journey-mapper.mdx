## What CLARA will ask you for

- **Programme name** — your programme's name (for example SKYPROTECT).
- **Track** — the part of the programme this document belongs to (for example a workstream, capability area or feature line), or `Programme-wide` if it spans several. The [CLARA page](/tools/clara) explains tracks.
- **Journey scope** — the journey being compared (for example "ICT cycle" or "Handling a contradiction-class alert").
- **PRD to compare against** — the PRD whose solution defines the AFTER column. Its scope table of product modules provides the wording for the AFTER steps.
- **Inputs** — CLARA will search your programme's Confluence space for the current-state journey (the BEFORE), the PRD whose solution defines the AFTER, the problem-impact analysis (to tag the phases), and optionally a persona. You can also paste in new material that is not in Confluence yet.

## Where the output lands

`Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Before-after-journeys/{{journey-scope}}` inside your programme's Confluence space.

## Tips

- This document is useful in two ways: as a picture for stakeholders of what changes, and as a coverage check. If a high-ranked problem has no AFTER, that points to a gap in the PRD, not in this page.
- Keep the sources strict: **BEFORE comes from the journey, AFTER comes from the PRD**. Once you write an AFTER step that the PRD does not cover, you are designing outside the PRD, which is the PRD generator's job.
- Step counts show *how* the work changes (handoffs removed, rebuilds avoided). They are not a measure of time. Cite the PRD's success measures for the real reduction, and say so clearly, so nobody reads the difference in steps as a benchmark.
- This is the last document in the chain (journey, then ranking, then PRD, then this one). If the before and after do not line up, do not fix it here. Correct the earlier document and generate this one again.
