## What CLARA will ask you for

- **Programme name** — your programme's name (for example SKYPROTECT).
- **Track** — the part of the programme this document belongs to (for example a workstream, capability area or feature line), or `Programme-wide` if it spans several. The [CLARA page](/tools/clara) explains tracks.
- **Outcome question** — what the research was trying to answer (one line, taken from the interview guide if there is one).
- **Inputs** — CLARA will search your programme's Confluence space for interview transcripts, field and exercise observation notes and operator session notes, and optionally a prior-knowledge summary on the topic and the interview guide used in the field. You can also paste in new material that is not in Confluence yet.

## Where the output lands

`Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Research-synthesis` inside your programme's Confluence space.

## Tips

- Themes are clusters, not summaries. If a "theme" is just one person's statement repeated, it is a quote, not a theme.
- The friction table is most useful when it is sorted. Unranked, it is a list of complaints. Ranked, it is a queue of actions.
- The problem statement is what separates a synthesis from notes. Resist leaving it open-ended: pick the framing the evidence best supports, and name the alternatives.
- Success criteria without a measure ("users should feel more confident") are not success criteria but hopes. Push for something like "X% of operators complete task Y in Z seconds without a second confirmation".
- This page is the single starting point for the rest of the Research phase. Later prompts (persona, journey, PRD and so on) read sections of this page directly, which is why all four sections live together.
