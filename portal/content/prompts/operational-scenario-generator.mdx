## What CLARA will ask you for

- **Programme name** — your programme's name (for example SKYPROTECT).
- **Track** — the part of the programme this document belongs to (for example a workstream, capability area or feature line), or `Programme-wide` if it spans several. The [CLARA page](/tools/clara) explains tracks.
- **Scenario title** — short (for example "Tank crew night transit through contested terrain", "Fighter aircraft low-altitude intercept" or "Frigate surface-contact classification at dusk").
- **Inputs** — CLARA will search your programme's Confluence space for operator research (interviews, field and exercise observations, and workshop outputs), a capability brief or statement of operational need, and optionally the doctrine or procedures the operators work from, themes and friction points. You can also paste in new material that is not in Confluence yet.

## Where the output lands

`Knowledge Base/{{track}}/Operational scenarios/{{scenario-title}}` inside your programme's Confluence space.

## Tips

- Check the draft with at least one operator before you use it in capability discussions. Their reaction is the real test.
- Pay particular attention to the failure modes. That is where design opportunities usually hide.
- Pair the output with a visual: use [Nanobanana](/tools/nanobanana), [Luma](/tools/luma-ai) or [Forma](/tools/autodesk-forma) to make the scenario tangible.
- The output feeds the [capability-spec generator](/prompts/capability-spec-generator) and the [mission-thread mapper](/prompts/mission-thread-mapper).
