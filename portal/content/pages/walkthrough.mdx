This tutorial takes a project from raw interview notes to a prototype that real users can test. You do not need a background in product management or design.

Use your own project. If you do not have one, use the **demo project**, SKYPROTECT, a made-up drone-defence programme. Step 4 sets it up. It comes with a brief from an earlier project, field notes and practice material for each tool. Prompts in this tutorial say SKYPROTECT. If you use your own project, replace it with your programme's name. Notes headed **For the demo project** apply only to the demo.

By the end you will have:

- **Research documents** in Confluence, each traced to its sources (steps 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12)
- **A requirements document** (PRD) (step 13)
- **A working prototype** built with PRIZM (step 14)
- **A test plan** (step 15)
- **A baseline and test results** to compare against (steps 6, 16)
- **A report card** that summarises your product in a standard format (step 17)

## 1. Check the prerequisites
::step{id="prerequisites" phase="Setup"}

Your research normally ends up scattered across notes, slides and chats.

**CLARA** is an AI assistant that turns your raw notes into structured documents, such as personas, journeys and a PRD, and files them in Confluence. Every claim links back to the note it came from, so you can check it. You make the decisions. CLARA does the drafting and the filing. CLARA builds on what you give it, so check each result before you move on.

Confirm you have the access you need before you start.

- A Confluence account with permission to create a space. Everything you produce is stored in a Confluence space.
- An AI assistant connected to Confluence, such as Claude with the Confluence connector turned on. Steps 2 and 4 use it.
- Access to [DASH](/tools/dash), at [diux-dash.com](https://diux-dash.com).
- **[Claude Code](/tools/claude-code) installed.** You need it for step 14, and for step 2 if you choose the file method.
- **Node.js 20 or later, and pnpm.** Claude Code uses them to install the prototype's packages in step 14. If your network is offline, ask your IT team for the address of your internal npm registry, then run `pnpm config set registry <address>`.
- **[INSIGHT](https://insight.vercel.app/) installed.** You need it for step 9, which is optional. The macOS and Windows apps are coming soon.
- Access to the CMS for [PULSE](https://pulse-dsta.vercel.app/). You need it for step 17. Ask your PULSE contact to add you.

::step{id="prior-knowledge" phase="Research" tool="CLARA"}

1. Paste this prompt into CLARA:
   ```prompt

   ```
2. Answer its questions about your programme name, track (or Programme-wide) and topic. Choose a specific topic, such as "how operators hand over between shifts", not "user research".

:::check

## 6. Measure how things are today
::step{id="baseline" phase="Research" tool="DASH"}

A **baseline** measures how things work today, so you can show later that your design is better. Do it before any interviews or design work.

1. Sign in at [diux-dash.com](https://diux-dash.com) and open your programme's workspace.
2. Create a session, choose **Baseline Testing**, and name the system people use today. For SKYPROTECT, that is the three-vendor toolchain of separate radar, RF and camera systems.

::screenshot{caption="The DASH new-session screen, with Baseline Testing selected" hint="the DASH new-session screen with Baseline Testing highlighted"}

3. Share the link or QR code with people who use that system. Responses appear in DASH as they answer. Keep this session open, because you compare against it in step 16.

::screenshot{caption="The Baseline Testing session, showing the share link and QR code" hint="the Baseline Testing session in DASH, showing the share link and QR code"}

DASH asks about **SUS** (System Usability Scale, a short score for how easy a system is to use), look and feel, user needs, system performance and **CSAT** (customer satisfaction). It also tracks **OKRs**, which are goals with numbers attached.

**For the demo project:** name the system **the current three-window toolchain: Halden Radar, Sigma RF Watch and Optix EO Viewer**. You have no operators to survey, so answer the survey yourself as a Site Alpha operator would. Use what you read in the kickoff brief. Ask a colleague to answer it too if you can, so you have more than one response to compare in step 16.

## 7. Review the product people use today
::step{id="heuristic-evaluation" phase="Research" tool="CLARA"}

A **heuristic evaluation** checks a product against a set of well-known usability rules, called **heuristics**. CLARA uses Nielsen's 10 heuristics. It scores the product out of 100 and lists each problem it finds, with a severity rating and the screen it appears on. The problems it finds feed your synthesis in step 10 and your ranking in step 12.

1. Collect the material to evaluate. Use numbered screenshots of the flows, a link CLARA can open, or a PDF or deck of the screens. For SKYPROTECT, that is the current radar, RF and camera systems.
2. Paste this prompt into CLARA:
   ```prompt
   Use CLARA's `heuristic-evaluator` for SKYPROTECT.
   ```
3. Tell CLARA the product name, then attach your material or paste the link.
4. Choose the evaluation mode: a competitor's product, an existing system you want to improve, a product being retired, or a standalone evaluation.
5. Name the one or two tasks to check, such as "an operator confirms and reports a threat". If you do not, CLARA proposes them from the screens and asks you to confirm.

::screenshot{caption="CLARA asking for the product, the evaluation mode and the tasks to check" hint="the AI assistant asking for the product name, the evaluation mode and the task scenarios, with the attached screenshots visible"}

6. Read the report. Check that each finding names a specific screen.

:::check
A report with a score out of 100, severity-rated findings that each cite a screen, and a fix list ranked P0, P1 and P2, filed under `Knowledge Base / [track] / Heuristic-evaluations / [product name]`.
:::

CLARA works from the material you give it. A link lets it check more states than screenshots do. One evaluator finds only about a third of the problems, so treat the findings as a minimum and the score as a ceiling. For a big decision, ask a second person to run the evaluation and merge the two.

**For the demo project:** download :file[skyprotect-demo-screens.zip]{href="/skyprotect-demo/skyprotect-demo-screens.zip"} and unzip it. It holds four numbered screenshots of the current toolchain: two of Halden Radar, one of Sigma RF Watch and one of Optix EO Viewer. Attach all four, then answer CLARA's questions like this:

```prompt
Product name: the current SKYPROTECT toolchain: Halden Radar, Sigma RF Watch and Optix EO Viewer
Evaluation mode: an existing system being improved
Task scenario: an operator confirms an incoming drone across the radar, RF and camera tools
```

:::details{title="Stuck? See fixes for this step."}
**If CLARA gives findings that do not cite a screen:**
- Ask it to number the screens and tie every finding to one. Delete any finding it cannot place.

**If the score looks too high or too low:**
- The score is for comparing: the same product over time, or competitors judged the same way. Do not read it as an absolute grade.
:::

## 9. Get live prompts in interviews
::step{id="interview-prompts" phase="Research" tool="INSIGHT"}

It runs on your computer without internet, and nothing leaves your device. This step is optional. It helps you run the conversations in step 8.

The macOS and Windows apps are listed as "coming soon" on the [INSIGHT site](https://insight.vercel.app/). Until they are released, run step 8 without it and carry on to step 10.

2. Press start at the beginning of the conversation. INSIGHT writes a transcript as you talk.
3. Read the **Now** card when you need a prompt. It shows one suggestion you can read aloud. A running summary and a glossary help you keep your place.

5. Export the review as Markdown and paste it into the **Field Notes** template, as in step 8.

::screenshot{caption="The session review with the export options" hint="the INSIGHT session review with the Markdown export option highlighted"}

A transcript with each speaker labelled, saved as a Markdown file and pasted into a Field Notes page in Confluence.

## 12. Rank the problems
::step{id="problem-ranking" phase="Research" tool="CLARA"}

Your research now lists many problems, and you cannot fix them all at once. CLARA ranks them so you know which one to build for first. It scores each problem on **reach** (how many people it affects), **severity** (how badly it hurts), **evidence** (how well it is proven) and **leverage** (whether fixing it also fixes others). Each score is out of 5, so a problem scores up to 20.

1. Paste this prompt into CLARA:
   ```prompt
   Use CLARA's `problem-impact-ranker` for SKYPROTECT.
   ```
2. Tell it which problems to rank, such as all problems across the journey, or one track's. Name where they come from, such as the friction table in your research synthesis from step 10, so the ranking can be traced.
3. Answer its questions where the evidence is thin. It does not invent evidence.
4. Read the ranked table and the tiers. Check that the top problems match what you saw in the research.

:::check
A ranked table with each problem scored out of 20, grouped into tiers. Each problem names whose pain it is and the evidence behind it, filed under `Knowledge Base / [track] / Problem-impact-analysis`.
:::

**For the demo project:** answer CLARA's questions like this:

```prompt
Programme name: SKYPROTECT
Track: Programme-wide
Problem set scope: all problems across confirming and reporting an incoming drone
Source of the problems: the friction points in the Programme-wide research synthesis, the heuristic evaluation and the field notes
```

CLARA may ask about the evidence for a need for an audit trail. The kickoff brief requires one, but S10 is the only adviser interviewed, and S10 could not say how often it matters. S08 mentions the handwritten log only in passing. Tell CLARA that. This is the kind of gap the ranking is meant to show.

:::details{title="Stuck? See fixes for this step."}
**If a persuasive problem ranks low:**
- Check its evidence. A problem backed only by discussion ranks below a duller one that people tested or saw in use. The gap tells you what to go and prove next.

**If CLARA asks for more problems or sources:**
- Point it to your research synthesis from step 10, or paste a list of pain points from a workshop.
:::

## 13. Write the requirements
::step{id="prd" phase="Research" tool="CLARA"}

A **PRD** (product requirements document) is one page everyone agrees on: the problem, who it is for, what the product must do and how you will know it worked. It is the most important output of Research.

1. Paste this prompt into CLARA:
   ```prompt
   Use CLARA's `prd-generator` for SKYPROTECT.
   ```
2. Choose the problem to build for. CLARA suggests the top-ranked problem from step 12.
3. Read the draft, then edit it by hand. It becomes the input for your prototype.

**For the demo project:** answer CLARA's questions like this:

```prompt
Programme name: SKYPROTECT
Track: Programme-wide
PRD title: Operator-console-v1
Original stakeholder ask: Operators at our two sites should not need three windows to confirm a drone. Commanders in the central ops room should be able to authorise faster. Show us a prototype that people can try.
```

Build for the problem of confirming a drone across the three tools and escalating it to the commander with the evidence. This is the kickoff ask. If your ranking puts another problem first, still build for this one, and note why in the PRD. The constraints in the kickoff brief, such as the 90-second window and the human decision, belong in the PRD.

## 17. Publish a report card
::step{id="report-card" phase="Test" tool="PULSE"}

A **report card** is a one-page summary of what your product does, what it costs, what it has achieved and who it has benefited. **PULSE** gives every product the same format, so products can be compared. Your AI assistant drafts the card from the material you already have, and you edit and publish it.

1. Open [PULSE](https://pulse-dsta.vercel.app/) and read the **Baseline** metrics that every report card includes, such as product stage, cost effectiveness, usability and satisfaction. Then read the **Domain** metrics for your area. For SKYPROTECT, that is Command and Control.
2. Open the PULSE CMS and copy its prompt.

::screenshot{caption="The prompt in the PULSE CMS, ready to copy" hint="the PULSE CMS with the report card prompt visible and the copy control highlighted"}

3. Paste the prompt into your AI assistant, then add your material: the PRD from step 13, your results from step 16 and the Knowledge Base pages that describe the product.
4. Paste the draft back into the CMS. It opens in the editor.
5. Check every figure against its source, edit the card by hand, then publish it.

::screenshot{caption="The published report card" hint="a published PULSE report card showing the baseline metrics and the domain metrics"}

PULSE separates data your team enters by hand, such as your DASH scores, from data it pulls automatically from the Telemetry Hub. Today you enter most of it yourself. Live data is on the PULSE roadmap.

**For the demo project:** SKYPROTECT is made up, so stop before you publish. Draft the card, read it and check each figure against its source, then discard it. Do not publish a made-up report card in PULSE.

:::check
A published report card that fills in the baseline metrics and the metrics for your domain, with each figure traced to a source you can name.
:::

:::details{title="Stuck? See fixes for this step."}
**If a metric is missing or does not fit your product:**
- Leave it blank rather than guess, then email the PULSE team using the contact link on the PULSE site.

**If the draft contains figures you cannot source:**
- Ask your assistant which document each figure came from. Delete any that it cannot name.
