## In short

Every team building software or capabilities for DSTA does three kinds of work:

1. **Deciding what to build, and whether it works for users.** This is **ProductOps**.
2. **Building, securing and shipping the software.** This is **DevSecOps**.
3. **Training, evaluating and deploying AI models.** This is **MLOps**.

## Why ProductOps comes first

The other two kinds of work build on what ProductOps decides. Without it, DevSecOps gets very good at delivering the wrong thing quickly, and MLOps gets very good at deploying models that change nothing for anyone.

## Working side by side, not one after another

Traditionally, the design side finishes and locks down its designs, then hands them to the engineering side to build. The Pipeline works the other way round.

Each pass through the flywheel produces **reference artefacts**: code, scenarios or visualisations that engineers can start from. (An artefact is a document or working model that the team produces.) The flywheel is BEACON's three-phase cycle of research, design and measurement, and it is explained on [its own page](/flywheel). Engineers can act on these reference artefacts while design is still going on.

So the hand-off is not a single moment. Work passes between the two sides all the time. This shortens the gap between what the team intends and how the product behaves, and it catches problems with fitting the parts together early, not at the end.

## Two loops at different speeds

The flywheel has three phases: Research, Design and Measure. They do not all turn at the same speed.

- **Research is the slow, outer loop.** The team does it thoroughly at the start of a programme or workstream. It sets what the team is trying to achieve. The team goes back to it only when it learns something that puts the original framing of the problem in doubt, not on every pass through Design and Measure.
- **Design and Measure make up the fast, inner loop.** The team builds something testable, tries it with users or operators, notes what it learns, and improves it. This is where most of the iterations happen.

What the team learns in Measure normally feeds the next Design pass. Occasionally the findings are strong enough to show that the problem framing itself has shifted. That is when the team goes back to Research, so the flywheel still closes. It just turns at different speeds in different places.

## Four design principles

Every tool, prompt and template in BEACON follows at least one of these principles.

1. **AI-native by design.** Every pass uses AI to cut manual effort and to bring out insights that used to need specialist time. BEACON works this way too, and every page offers prompts you can paste into an AI tool.
2. **Built for both domains from the start (dual-domain).** The Pipeline serves both digital programme teams and engineering programme teams. The two share infrastructure first, with domain-specific tools only where their work really differs.
3. **No specialist skills needed.** You give instructions in plain language. A developer or a programme manager should be able to use the tools without first becoming a product professional.
4. **Build where it creates unique value; buy where industry has solved it.** DSTA builds its own tools for its own unique context. Where an off-the-shelf product (COTS) already solves a general problem, it uses that.
