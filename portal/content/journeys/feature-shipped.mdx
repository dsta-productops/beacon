---
title: "I have a finished feature — what now?"
scenario: "A team has shipped something and wants to know whether it is working. The instinct is to celebrate, or to move on to the next thing. The Pipeline asks: did it actually achieve what it was meant to?"
order: 6
visibility: "public"
steps:
  - label: "Restate the success criteria"
    description: "Go back to the PRD or capability specification. What did the team say good would look like? The criteria should be measurable and about what the product had to be able to do, and to what level. If they were vague, that is a finding in itself."
  - label: "Run a system survey in DASH"
    description: "Collect structured feedback from real users or operators of the deployed system (the system in live use). If the team recorded a baseline at the start of the project, compare against it. That turns \"the new system scored X\" into \"the new system improved on the old one by Y\"."
    tool: "dash"
  - label: "Instrument with Dynatrace"
    description: "Quantitative usage data (numbers) on the live product: paths, drop-offs, error rates and anomalies. Pair them with the DASH survey results. What people do, plus what they say, is stronger than either alone."
    tool: "dynatrace"
  - label: "Run usability sessions (digital) or operator validation (engineering)"
    description: "Quantitative evidence (numbers) tells you what happened. Qualitative evidence (what users say and what you observe) tells you why. You need both."
  - label: "Synthesise the findings"
    description: "What changed because of this work? What did not? What surprised the team?"
  - label: "Decide: iterate, harden or sunset"
    description: "Iterate means keep improving the product, harden means make it more robust, and sunset means retire it. A finding with no decision attached is a finding that changed nothing."
  - label: "Update OKRs in DASH"
    description: "When the results confirm or rule out a target, update the OKRs (goals with measurable targets) in DASH so the next round measures against the right thing. This is how the Pipeline builds up measurement discipline over time."
    tool: "dash"
  - label: "Update the PRD or scenario"
    description: "Write what the team learned into the source document, so the next team benefits. This is how the Pipeline builds up knowledge."
---

Shipping is not the end of the iteration. Measure is. A feature that has been delivered but not measured has not been confirmed as working.

This journey shows how to close the loop on something already in use: collect honest evidence, and feed what you learn into the next round of the flywheel.
