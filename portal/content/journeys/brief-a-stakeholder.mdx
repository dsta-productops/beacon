title: "I need to brief a new stakeholder"
scenario: "Someone new is joining the conversation, such as a senior leader, another team's lead or a partner team. They need to get up to speed without reading every document the team has produced."
order: 5
visibility: "public"
steps:
  - label: "Pull the research synthesis"
    description: "The one-page version: who the team is building for, what problem is being solved, and what success looks like. Written in plain language, without jargon."
    prompt: "research-synthesiser"
    tool: "clara"
  - label: "Pull the current PRD or operational scenario"
    description: "This is what the team is building, not what it hopes to build. Show it as it stands today, not after the next planned revision."
  - label: "Show the most recent thing tested with users"
    description: "Digital teams: the interactive prototype. Engineering teams: the storyboard or virtual prototype. Use whatever the team most recently put in front of users or operators. Give links, not screenshots."
  - label: "Show the most recent test results"
    description: "DASH survey data, observation findings and OKR status (OKRs are goals with measurable targets). Be honest about what is working and what is not. The briefing is more credible when it includes failures."
    tool: "dash"
  - label: "Name the two or three open questions"
    description: "Where the team really needs input. A briefing with no open questions tells the new stakeholder there is nothing for them to contribute. Make sure that is actually true before you say it."
  - label: "Send them a bundle of links, not a deck"
    description: "Slides go stale the moment they are sent. Links to live documents (the synthesis page, the PRD, the prototype link and the DASH workspace) stay current as the project changes. The stakeholder can come back at any time and see where things stand."
---

When briefing someone new, the temptation is to build a deck that tells the whole story from start to finish. That deck takes a day to write, pulls the team away from the work, and is out of date by the next round.

The work below is about putting together documents the team has already produced. You link to them, you do not write them again. The briefing is honest about open questions and ongoing failures. It is a snapshot of where the team is, not a pitch for where it is going.
