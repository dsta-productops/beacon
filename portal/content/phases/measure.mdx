## What this phase is for

Measure is where the work meets reality. The aim is to gather evidence on whether what you built delivers the outcomes you intended, and to feed that evidence into the next Design round fast enough to act on it.

For both digital and engineering teams, the tool for measuring is DASH. The type of survey depends on how mature the product is. A prototype survey runs right after a rapid-prototype session. A system survey runs after a system in use has been used long enough to give a real-world signal.

When test results conclusively confirm or refute a target, the team updates the OKRs (goals with measurable targets) in DASH, and publishes what the product has achieved on a PULSE report card.

## How Measure relates to Design and Research

Measure is the other half of the inner loop. Each Design round produces something testable, Measure tests it, and the findings drive the next Design adjustment. The findings live in DASH, as measurements, survey responses and OKR updates, for design teams to pick up and apply in the next round.

The team goes back to Research only when findings are strong enough to show that the problem itself has changed. That is what starts the outer loop again.
