## What this phase is for

Design turns needs you have confirmed into something you can test: concrete enough to react to, with the right amount of detail for the question you are asking. The goal is not a finished product. It is something a team can put in front of users or operators in the next Measure round.

Throughout this page, an **artefact** means a document or working model that the team produces, such as a prototype.

- **Digital teams** build prototypes quickly with Claude Code and PRIZM, DSTA's design system. The prototype is itself the reference code: working code that follows the design system, so the engineering side (DevSecOps) can pick it up alongside.
- **Engineering teams** make virtual prototypes (rendered fly-throughs or walk-throughs of how a capability would work), capability storyboards (step-by-step scripts of how it is used) and system diagrams. Operators can engage with these before the team commits to buying a costly platform.

## How Design relates to Research and Measure

Design takes as its input what Research produced: the PRD for digital teams, and the operational scenario and capability specification for engineering teams. It does not write to the Knowledge Base. Its outputs live in code repositories, hosted prototype links, design tools and rendered scenario files.

Design is one half of the inner loop. Each Design round produces something to test. Measure tests it, the findings come back, and Design improves it. The loop runs quickly, and most of a programme's iterations happen here.
